<p>AI is now part of everyone's workflow, mine included, so writing code is no longer what sets a developer apart. What matters is who understands your store, tests the change before it goes live, and takes responsibility if something goes wrong. That is trust and accountability, and no tool provides them.</p>

<h2>Trust is built over time</h2>
<p>When you work with the same developer for months or years, they learn how your store actually runs: which apps you depend on, which pages bring in revenue, what you changed last quarter and why. You do not have to explain it again each time. An AI tool starts every conversation with no memory of your store, your history or your priorities, so you end up re-explaining the same context and hoping nothing important is missed.</p>

<h2>Accountability means someone owns the result</h2>
<p>If a change breaks your checkout at 9 a.m. on a sale day, you need a person who picks up the message, takes responsibility and fixes it. An AI tool cannot be held to anything. It will not call you back, it will not apologize, and it will not notice a week later that its suggestion quietly broke your product schema. With a developer, the outcome is their name on the work.</p>

<h2>What a long-term relationship gives you</h2>
<ul>
  <li><b>Faster fixes.</b> Familiarity with your theme means problems are found in minutes, not hours.</li>
  <li><b>Honest advice.</b> Sometimes the right answer is "you do not need this change." A good developer says so.</li>
  <li><b>Consistency.</b> Code, naming and styling stay tidy instead of becoming a patchwork of pasted snippets.</li>
  <li><b>Fewer surprises.</b> Changes are applied on a duplicate theme first, reviewed by you, and only then go live.</li>
</ul>

<h2>AI as a tool, not a replacement</h2>
<p>I do not see AI and a developer as competitors. Used well, AI speeds up routine work, and I pass that saving on in turnaround time. But the judgment about what to change, the testing, and the responsibility for the outcome stay with me. That combination is what you are really paying for.</p>
